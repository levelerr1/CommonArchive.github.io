>How does migration affect a country’s growth?
>
*“How do you presume immigration will affect a country’s future”*, I asked variations of this question to multiple friends and got varying answers, but all had a common underlying idea ***”No one likes their territory invaded”***. But is this really the mindset we should have?
Following the recent surge in xenophobic movement across countries this year, I wanted to understand if this was a progressive mindset, not why migration should or should not be encouraged? Is aversion to migration an ideal approach? For the sake of this curiosity of mine I will present arguments on why people hate foreigners.
The word *xenophobia* is made up of two elements: *”xeno”* which means *foreign*, and *”phobia”* which translates to *hate*. The hate of foreigners. Why do South Africans oppose the presence of other African citizens working in their country? Easy! They believe resources and opportunities are being used up by foreigners and leaving little to them, —I must say this, this piece is not a political piece. It is not meant to oppose policies or serve as an expression of political views, it is just an insight. The current ICE (*Immigration and Customs Enforcement*) objectives also share the same idea of *“They are taking or jobs”*. Now, from a superficial perspective this could appear true, and has often be utilized by right winged/ conservative candidates to augment their political agendas. We saw this ideology utilized by the *Nazi* Germany, communist party China, and even in North Korea —which current political group is still ruling. USAFacts.com in 2025 reported that about 5.6 million foreign born workers work in education and health services. NCCR —I forgot the meaning of this group’s abbreviation and will edit this later if a request is made— reports that most migrants, about 70%, work in the service field, this includes construction, cleaners, healthcare, cleaners, helpers an as well as plasterers, to name a few.  Foreign born workers make up approximately 18.6% of the U.S. workforce. 1 in 5 workers is an immigrant. From this statistics alone, it is difficult to justify the apprehensiveness of citizens concerning foreigners —I must have said the word foreigner about 15 times now, I am not counting— but if we look at, let’s say, statistics from the year 1950, it shows that value (18.6%) was just 6.2% 76 years ago. That is nearly a 3× increase in the percentage volume that migrant workers occupy. So yes, there appear to be an increasing trend in the influx of migrants, but that is expected amidst the child boom of the late 20th/21st century, and this is not only a trend that is present in the U.S., Sweden, for example, had about 5% of migrant workers in 1970 and today that value is 11%, so there is definitely an uptick. Migration surges have always geared toward developed countries.
But the question remains, *”Does immigration negatively affect a country’s growth?*”. The way I see it, no one is going to migrate to a war torn region just because its housing is cheaper nor move to an eroded region because it is under crowded. As evidenced by repeating patterns, people migrate to more prosperous regions, such that we see a direct correlation between migration and wealth. The more developed a country get, the more people desire to move there. This is expected. Migration acts as a marker that signals the growth of a region, state, country, telling people to pay attention to a specific region, making them also in turn desire it, conduct business in that region. Increasing immigration of high skilled workers contribute greatly to a country’s output.

Then, there are controversies usually associated with migration such as, the cultural differences and values, societal tolerance level, violence, these all cause frictions amongst societies, and truth be done, it is much easier to blame a migrant for the misfortunes of a society. We see media outlets capitalizing on this, crimes reported by the media showing or involving migrants as the perpetrators often receive more outcry from the public and spread faster, rallying public interest to most media outlets, it is all about making ends meet.  
Humans often migrate, it is all we do, it is an innate tendency; it helps us grow; it is responsible for our genetic diversity. It comes with its own disadvantage, like everything does. The way I see it, migration signals advancement and if not moderated, the perks that attracted migrants would be lost. I also believe irrational opposition to immigration is destructive, isolating and an overall primal response to change.
One should have a degree of gladness if people perceive their country as a desirable environment build a home rather than a place to visit for tourism of for sights.
