> What makes the Ukraine-Russia war seem different from the Israel-Palestine conflict?

When I was younger, I frequently interacted with kids who were much larger than I am, and sometimes they 
are inclined to intimidate me. It came in multiple forms, verbal insults, attempts at extortion, teasing,
but rarely does it escalate into something significant and as such, for the most part, I under-perceived 
the detrimental effects of bullying, as I did not feel the peer critic that accompanies being nerdy. As I
grew older, I observed the intensity of the bullying shown in TV shows; it always showed atrocious and 
questionable acts committed by school bullies that are not usually proportional to their age range and
the notion always stood out to me. Are these bullies portrayed never scared of the repercussions?

In public school in *Lagos state* circa 2012-2016, being caught or even perceived as a bully came with 
significant consequences that would be considered as borderline child abuse. A reason for this is due to
a combination of Lagos state teaching services intolerance to bullying and truancy, teacher’s culture and
the overall tradition. An example is the use of corporal punishment in schools as a form of corrective measures
and teachers generally have little patience for any acts of insubordination.

On the *24th of February,* 2022, the Russian military launched a full scale invasion into Ukraine, attacking 
multiple directions simultaneously with the aim of capturing *Kyiv*, highlighting their opposition the 
involvement of NATO (*North Atlantic Treaty Organization*) especially U.S military troops —the U.S being
a member then— citing the need to claim Ukraine as a matter of national security. This saw a quick polarization 
in the military presence in Ukraine as the war advanced. NATO played a major role in role in the war, which was
ironic when considering this was what Russia campaigned against and threatened the utilization of nuclear warheads.
The war advanced and this lead to interesting innovations in modern warfare especially in terms of the mass 
adoption of fibre-optic drones and other contraptions in the war field. In many ways this conflict appears 
like bullying, a large country with vast resources and manpower encroaches on a relatively smaller country and
thus is not just a disparity in land sizes, it is a disparity in pure brute force. We can see a similarity between
the *Russian-Ukraine* war and the *Israel-Palestine* conflict. Now, usually in the Middle East the frequency of 
hostile activities between regions has been on the higher part of the margin, but the Israel/Palestine conflict
stands out because of the contrast between both countries firepower is large. Now, I want to make it clear to my
readers that I am not debating the justification of any conflict but rather why they are perceived and in this case,
why it is perceived as bullying.

![ICBM]({{ site.baseurl }}/assets/images/icbm.jpeg)

Bullying according to *Dan Olweus*, in his book *Bullying at School*, identifies bullying as not just being *mean*,
but highlights three components involved in bullying: *Intentional aggression, repetition (persistence) and power imbalance*.
We see this, especially the last two, in these conflicts; Israel for example has had multiple encounters with the Palestine
military force with aggressive action repeated multiple times. The disparity in fire power between countries highlighted
has already been discussed earlier —in the case of the *Russia-Ukraine* war, this is most glaring when the war started. 
But there is central narrative *Olweus* enforces, “*the passerby*”. *Olweus* insists that individual who observe bullying 
in progress, or was notified about it, and take no actions to mitigate it, have enabled the bullying. Now, this is a 
convoluted ideology and I am unable to qualify the *Russian-Ukraine* war as bullying based on it, for even though at 
the start of the war Russia had more brute force, nonetheless multiple countries sanctioned the actions of Russia. 
The Palestinian conflict on the other hand did not receive a global action that supported Palestine until recently 
when the strain of the conflict spiked, and as such the criticism regarding the contrast in firepower received minor 
attention.

We could also perceive the enabling of bullying by reviewing funding allocated to each country (especially countries 
that have a lesser financial, economic and military repertoire), Ukraine for example has been a major beneficiary of 
military budgets and aid allocations from NATO countries. Infact, even after the withdrawal of the U.S from NATO. 
On the other hand, around 2004, most countries have ceased military aid to the Palestine forces —it is important to
know that this was a result of multiple demands and ceasefire being broken by both sides and the *Hamas* refusing to
acknowledge the existence of Israel as a sovereign state— and by comparison, Israel —which has way better funding— 
gradually is perceived as a global superpower brute-forcing its way on the territory of a much smaller country’s 
territory by just sheer force rather than negotiations and tactics, and this makes the conflict appear one-sided 
with Palestine bearing more casualties. This is not helped by the prevalence if lobbying groups for the support of
Israel existing in first world countries, an example of this is AIPAC (*American Israel Public Affairs Committee*). 
As such, considering the amount of support, firepower and military allocation that Israel has, we can understand why
the public opinion has progressively shifted over the years and presents Israel as a bully, but then opposite is 
observed in the *Russian-Ukraine* war. Russia is also perceived as an aggressor but because the Ukrainian military 
has been able to provide counter attacking measures, the idea of Russia winning through sheer becomes unlikely; as
such, even though Russia had fit into the bully ideology at the start of the war, the amount of support Ukraine 
received over the years in a relatively short period invalidates Russia as a bully. Bully the, not bully now.

Generally, my perception of bullying is biased as I have not had much experience with bullies, especially in 
extreme forms, but I will say this, *Bullying thrives in environments that encourage it*. Regardless of political
motivations behind each conflict, it is easy to see why powerful countries are often portrayed as bullies in public
opinions. The difference between right and wrong all comes down to biases.
